## Sub-Topic 1 - Dark Design Patterns

Research here was conducted on Dark Design Patterns and the unethical practices it creates. These are things to understand and avoid as a developer.

# Key findings:

- Dark design patterns are unethical applications of UX knowledge designed to cheat or manipulate users—such as making it unnecessarily difficult to cancel a subscription or tricking users into buying unwanted items. False Urgency, Basket Sneaking, Subscription Traps, and Confirmshaming harm the consumer immensely.

- Many dark patterns are simply psychological principles taken too far. It's no longer a debate as some governments are stepping in and outright banning certain dark design patterns and punishing companies that persist with harmful design.

## Sub-Topic 2 - Laws of UX

Research here was conducted on UX designs and principles. Things to keep in mind when someone is developing an application on the frontend and how to keep users actively engaged.

# Key findings:

- Users spend the majority of their time on other websites and apps, meaning they have already developed expectations for how things should work. Rather than reinventing the wheel, designers should leverage these existing mental models so users can focus on their tasks rather than learning a new interface.

- Reducing Cognitive Load is important, the time to make a decision increases with more choices. Chunking information and keeping options simple reduces the stress and user may experience.

<br>

## Sub-Topic 3 - 15 Rules for Creating Web Apps

Research here was conducted on Design guidelines when creating user-friendly interfaces. It's less about aesthetics and moreso about raw usablity and how easily it can be adopted into the market.

# Key findings:

- Don't Reinvent the Wheel; by relying on established UI patterns such as standard icons, and familiar layouts , you'll keep users engaged as oppposed confusing the users with new navigation or brand new untested ideas.

- Limit Features to Prevent Clutter; The desire to constantly add new features can lead to a bloated, confusing product. Keeping an app simple and focused on its core utility prevents new users from getting overwhelmed by unnecessary buttons and options.
