## Sub-Topic 1 - Workflow

Research was conducted regarding the general workflow of working on Github, setting up milestones, issues, and project boards.

# Key findings:

- 1: Creating multiple branches is an industry standard, this keeps work separated and organized in isolated workflows, rather than sending everything to the "Main" branch. PRs (Pull Requests) should attached to milestones and related issues as well so that anyone who looks at the work is aware of what tasks you may have been working on.

- 2: Project boards are adaptable tables, boards and roadmaps that intergrate issues and pull requests. They can help with tracking and planning work as they're synced to your projects as you make changes. There are also different templates that can be used based on preference, and start/end dates can be added to issues and milestones to set timelines for one dev, or a team of developers.

<br>

## Sub-Topic 2 - Using Branches

Research here was conducted on brnaches, their use cases and overall importance in the development workflow.

# Key findings:

- 1: Creating a branch allows devs to encapsulate any and all new features, bug fixes, or experiments in an isolated environment. This keeps unstable code away from the main project history until it is fully ready to be merged.

- 2: Branches incredibly lightweight, movable pointers to a specific commit, this makes creating and destroying brnaches nearly instantaneous.

<br>

## Sub-Topic 3 - Comparing Workflows

Research here was conducted on Git Workflows, tips and tricks for what makes a successful workflow, and other popular workflows.

# Key findings:

- 1: There's no "One Size Fits All" workflow, they're guidelines, but they're not rules. The best workflow is one that enhances a team's productivity without causing any stress.

- 2: Prioritizing short-lived branches reduces the risk of merge conflicts and deployment challenges regardless of the workflow.

- 3: advanced workflows such as Feature branching a Gitflow promote isolating development work to keep the main branch clean and prevents the need to fix and/or revert broken merges.
