## Topic - Structure

This document contains general notes related to structuring a project, thinking about the user interface and in turn using low-fidelity wireframes as mockups to put those ideas to form.

<br>

## Sub-Topic 1 - What is wireframing?

Research was conducting on the concept of wireframing and how it acts as the basic blueprint or "skeleton" for an application or website. It can outline layouts, components, and user flows without all the polish.

# Key findings:

- Fidelity drives the feedback you get, if stakeholders get nitpicky about minor visual details like colors or aesthetic polish, it may be a good idea to lower fidelity and focus on core functionality and user needs. Keep it simple.

- When dealing with more complex UI, ditch the "Lorem Ipsun" placeholder text. It's fine for early stages, but when content-rich applications are in play, it far better to use actual content to test a pages information architecture.

<br>

## Sub-Topic 2 - Things to avoid

Research was conducted on the importance of wireframing for a responsive website, changing the way we do wireframing as a whole, and things to avoid when doing so.

# Key findings:

- Don't sweat the details. When wireframing, it's best not overly emphazsize color and design, it's best to primarily work in grayscale to focus on function and limit distraction. Details can always be added later into the process if need be.

- Don't wireframe every single page. While it's always good to know the application's content ahead of time and what toyur user is trying to achieve, not every page needs to be laid out. It's better to focus on the core elements.

- Brdge the gap between teams. Wireframes are not just blueprints, they can be an invaluable communication tool that gives devs a clear picture of what needs to be coded. Every change should also be communicated as well.

<br>

## Sub-Topic 3 - How to design an app

Research was conducted on the creation process of an application, and some things to think about before even touching any design tools.

# Key findings:

- Understand the "Who and Why". Before anything else, it's best to know the exact problem the application solves and who it's designed for. Creating an application without that core understanding leads to wasted time and fustration.

- Prototype over code. Building prototypes or rough drafts allows for planning of navigation, user flow, and test interactions early.
